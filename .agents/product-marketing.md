# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-07-31

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Satoshi Web builds real apps (on the App Store) and custom systems for local businesses — not just websites.
**What it does:** An app & systems studio that (a) builds custom mobile apps and automation systems for individual local businesses, and (b) ships productized vertical SaaS per niche (Ordofy for nightlife venues; apps for driving schools and security in the pipeline). The website is now an add-on, not the core offer.
**Product category:** App development + vertical SaaS for local businesses (moving off the commoditized "web agency" shelf).
**Product type:** Hybrid — service (custom apps/systems) + SaaS products (Ordofy, etc.).
**Business model:** Mix — one-off/project for custom builds + recurring (retainer/subscription) for apps & SaaS. Goal: shift weight toward recurring revenue.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Local businesses in Italy (start: Civitavecchia → expand nationally). Owner-run, small-to-mid size.
**Decision-makers:** The owner (single decision-maker, emotional + ROI-driven).
**Primary use case:** Modernize a business still run on paper/phone/WhatsApp — get a real app/system that brings customers, automates manual work, and removes operational chaos.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Get more customers / revenue without more manual effort
- Stop drowning in manual ops (bookings, orders, WhatsApp, payments)
- Own a premium, differentiated digital presence (a real app, on the App Store)
**Use cases:** Nightlife venues (QR bottle/table ordering + staff app = Ordofy); driving schools (bookings/lessons/students app); security firms; custom apps for any local business.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Local businesses lose customers and time because their operations run on paper, phone calls and WhatsApp — and a generic website doesn't fix operations.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Generic web agencies sell commoditized websites (a flyer, not a system)
- Enterprise SaaS (SevenRooms etc.) is too expensive/complex, US-centric, English-only
- DIY tools require time/skill the owner doesn't have
**What it costs them:** Lost customers, wasted hours, revenue leaking to platforms (e.g., Booking commissions), errors.
**Emotional tension:** Feeling behind, overwhelmed, unsure who to trust, afraid of wasting money.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct (Ordofy / nightlife):** SevenRooms, UrVenue, GuestBuddy, Mr. Black, Booking Ninjas — strong & funded, but target large venues, US/international, enterprise pricing, English. Fall short for small Italian venues (overkill, costly, not local).
**Secondary (services):** Generic web/agency shops — sell websites, not systems; commoditized, race-to-bottom pricing. Can't publish real App Store apps.
**Indirect:** DIY (Wix/manual/WhatsApp) — cheap but doesn't scale, keeps the owner doing manual work.
**Moat:** Verified Apple Developer → real apps published on the App Store. Few local competitors can/do this.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Real apps on the App Store (Apple Developer moat) — not just websites
- Local + Italian + hands-on service vs distant enterprise SaaS
- Productized per niche (speaks the vertical's language) + custom builds
- Unique high-value add-ons nobody offers (e.g., NFC Google-review cards)
**How we do it differently:** We sell the outcome/system (more revenue, less chaos), not "a website" or "an app."
**Why that's better:** An app is a relationship (on the customer's phone: push, loyalty, bookings), a website is a flyer.
**Why customers choose us:** Differentiated (App Store), local trust, results-focused, benefit-led.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Do I even need an app?" | Sell the outcome, not "an app". Show the ready system for their niche + a real result (case study). |
| "Too expensive" | Frame ROI: the system pays for itself in X extra customers/less manual work; recurring, not just a cost. |
| "Who are you? / can I trust you?" | Apple Developer verified, real case studies (Lazzi +38%, IKTA 30 countries), Google reviews, real products (Ordofy/Axis). |

**Anti-persona:** Price-shoppers who want the cheapest possible one-off website; businesses with no interest in growth/ops.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Manual chaos, lost customers, commissions to platforms, a dead/old website.
**Pull:** A real app + system that works 24/7; differentiation; "finally someone who gets it."
**Habit:** "We've always done it on paper/WhatsApp; it works ok."
**Anxiety:** Fear of cost, of it being complicated, of wasting money on a young/unknown provider.
